**Professional Self-Assessment**
	
   Throughout my time in the Computer Science program and through the development of my ePortfolio, I have gained a strong understanding of both foundational and advanced concepts 
that have shaped my professional strengths and clarified my long-term career direction. Completing the capstone and revisiting prior work pushed me to re-examine how my technical 
skills, problem-solving strategies, and personal values intersect with the expectations of the computer science field. This portfolio is not only a collection of completed coursework 
– it represents my growth as a developer, my ability to reflect critically on my work, and my readiness to apply these skills in real-world environments such as data engineering, 
software development, and related analytical roles. 
	
   One of the most impactful aspects of my academic experience has been learning to collaborate effectively within team environments. Several courses required coordinated development,
version control workflows, code reviews, and problem-solving as a group. These experiences helped me build communication skills that are essential when designing systems, conveying 
technical information to non-technical stakeholders, and aligning project goals within a team. Throughout the program, I strengthened my ability to translate complex concepts – such 
as API integrations, data flow logic, or architectural decisions – into concise and accessible explanations. These communication skills are highlighted in my capstone code review video, 
where I walk through system architecture, enhancements, and reasoning with clarity and confidence.
	
   The program also provided extensive experience in data structures and algorithms, which fundamentally shaped how I design solutions. Assignments involving recursion, sorting, search 
strategies, graph structures, and optimized data processing helped me develop analytical thinking and performance-aware design habits. These skills directly informed the enhancements I 
implemented in my CS360 mobile application, such as rolling averages, trend detection algorithms, and more structured state management. Learning to reason about time complexity, 
efficiency trade-offs, and algorithmic correctness has made me a more disciplined and thoughtful developer. 
	
   My coursework in software engineering exposed me to design principles such as separation of concerns, test-driven development, modular architecture, and user-centered design. 
Implementing Android applications, building CRUD-based systems, and refactoring legacy code trained me to approach software systematically. Similarly, studying databases – both 
relational and NoSQL – gave me practical experience in schema design, normalization, transactional operations, and secure data handling. In my CS320 project, I extended an originally 
in-memory testing framework into a MongoDB-backed persistence layer, demonstrating my ability to integrate modern data solutions while preserving validation logic and application integrity.
	
   Security was another consistent theme across the program. From validating input fields in service layers to understanding access control models, encryption basics, and threat modeling,
I developed a mindset focused on preventing vulnerabilities before they occur. This approach influenced the enhancements I made to all artifacts, ensuring they follow best practices for 
error handling, safe data manipulation, and defensive programming.
